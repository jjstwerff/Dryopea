# Reading by goal — where to start, and what it will cost you to skip

Extracted from `CLAUDE.md` so the always-loaded file stays a router.
Its § Reading by goal carries the same goals with their DESTINATIONS
only; this file carries the destination **and the argument** — the
warning, the measurement and the decision code behind each row.

⚠ **Look a row up before doing the thing it names.**  Most rows exist
because somebody did the thing without reading, and the warning is what
that cost.  A row's ⚠ marks are load-bearing: `⚠⚠` is a claim that has
already been got wrong at least once in this repo.

⚠ **The owning DOCUMENT is the source of truth, never this row.**  A row
is a navigational summary, and a summary drifts — when the two disagree,
the document a row points at is right and the row is stale.

| Ask why a BUILD accelerant cannot be priced, or propose one | ⚠⚠ **`@X022`'s recon window is FREE AND UNLIMITED** (`@M092`) — the wave list does not start until the player pokes a spawn marker, so a second builder before wave 1 saves the player's **wall-clock** and not one game-tick, and a build accelerant has nothing to accelerate against.  ⚠⚠ **The pressure `@X024` describes is the PERMIT's** (§ X2d, `@X025`, [`plans/ROADMAP.md`](../plans/ROADMAP.md) item **8**), which makes item 8 the missing INCENTIVE rather than a follow-on: **9 before 8 for the CONTENT, 8 for the reason to go NOW** |
| Author what a WAVE IS MADE OF | `schedule 4 12` arms the list, `compose 1 4 miner 8 scout` says what one wave of it is made of ([plan 23](../plans/23-the-small-robots/README.md) K1, `@X056`).  ⚠ **`compose` REPLACES a wave and a later `schedule` line WIPES it**, so the order `emit.loft` writes is a requirement, not a style.  ⚠ A wave's SIZE is SUMMED from its parts and never stored (`@X055`), so `schedule 12` + `compose 0 3 miner 2 scout` is a wave of **five** — there is no total to disagree with.  ⚠⚠ **The ORDER you write is worth NOTHING** (plan 23 K3, `@M018`) — it sets the departure order, and since K2b the faster class overtakes, so four scouts first, four scouts LAST and four scouts alternated all land on the same tick.  K0's *"order is worth 20x"* was measured on enemies PLACED at different distances, before classes had speeds.  ⚠⚠ **What a mix IS worth is its FASTEST member and nothing else** — four harvesters in front of eight miners behaves like twelve harvesters, not like anything in between — so write compositions expecting the quickest class to decide the outcome.  ⚠ `examples/waves.json` is NOT the place — `WaveFile` deliberately has no composition (`@X057`) |
| Ask what CLOCKS a run, or why the player must be efficient | [`EXPLORATION.md`](EXPLORATION.md) § X2d — the **permit**, and it is the cordon's own rule rather than a timer bolted on for pressure: *"they have a time window before they need to get back into space"*.  ⚠ Expiry must not be a fail screen — it costs the CARGO, which is what `DESIGN.md` § 14 already says a bad run looks like.  ⚠⚠ **How the player TRACKS it is MEASURED and still the owner's to rule** (`@X287`, `@M064`): `@X250` proposed the cordon's battleships as the clock, and **the game's default frame contains no sky** — the follow camera stops 0.96° below the horizon, fourteen pixels.  Three answers survive; the recommendation is a passing SHADOW on the ground.  ⚠ And none is buildable until the SCRAMBLE, or the clock is a number nothing reads |

| Judge a POWER, AUTOMATION or TRANSPORT idea (cables, rails, conveyors, autopilot) | ⚠⚠ **It is almost certainly refused, and the refusal is MEASURED** — [`docs/MATERIALS.md`](MATERIALS.md) § Power (`@X104`, `@X105`).  A wire or a rail replaces a DRIVE, and [`plans/17`](../plans/17-tower-hot-swap/README.md) § T3 measured the drive as the game: two *shuttling* helpers clear all 205 robots where the same two *parked* reach 5/7 and the base falls.  ⚠ The ENEMY may have the full grid — `ROBOT_ECONOMY.md` § 3 already makes a route a thing to cut, which adds a decision instead of deleting one |
| Ask why the player must be physically present, or why there is no remote control | [`docs/SETTING.md`](SETTING.md) § The recruitment (`@X099`) — ⚠⚠ **the charter of the whole game, and it is one sentence of dialogue**: the multi-frequency jammer that makes the robots beatable is what makes communication with the surface impossible, *"so there has to be personnel below to service the different systems"*.  ⚠ Every servicing loop the project has measured exists because of it, which is why a comms upgrade that works under the scrambler is refused |
| Ask why machines DECAY, or why the map is littered with wrecks nobody killed | [`docs/SETTING.md`](SETTING.md) § The pollen (`@X100`) — aggressive to humans **and machines**, so repair is a *condition* rather than an event.  ⚠ It supplies the cause for four systems designed separately: the robots' repair points, why an approaching robot reads as helpful, free salvage with no combat attached, and base upkeep.  ⚠ It must stay a CONSTANT — a pollen *storm* the player waits out is a lean-back mechanic |
| Judge a KNOWLEDGE, lore-unlock or discovery idea | [`docs/SETTING.md`](SETTING.md) § The knowledge tree (`@X102`) — ~55 facts in per-faction arcs, recovered from the 2023 game data.  ⚠⚠ **The boundary is one line**: knowledge changes what the player can **ATTEMPT** and what the map will SHOW them, never how well they do it.  *Cave entrances* makes a scan exist; it does not make scanning better.  ⚠ A knowledge item granting *+10% hacking* is a stat wearing a story's clothes — it belongs on [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2's crew axis instead.  ⚠⚠ And knowledge IS the answer, so it must be **found and never sold** (`@X118`) |
| Judge a PROGRESSION idea (upgrades, unlocks, XP, stats) | ⚠⚠ **[`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) was REWRITTEN 2026-08-26 and the old answer is inverted** — it used to say *the progression is the player's skill* and refuse stats; the owner ruled otherwise (`@X016`/`@X017` superseded, `@X103` reversed).  ⚠ The model is **Blue Prince** (`@X116`): what the player KNOWS and DECIDES dominates (`@X117`), **and there is a lot of room for friction-reducers** — build speed, repair speed, detection radius.  ⚠⚠ The test is `@X118`: **does this reduce the cost of acting on what the player knows, or does it supply what they do not know?**  A shortcut saves you steps; it never tells you the combination |
| Ask why a stat or an upgrade is ALLOWED now | [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P6 — the fence moved and narrowed.  ⚠ It forbids **doing the player's thinking** (a recommended layout, a readout that NAMES the incoming composition, salvage without the trip) and **a route to winning by personal power** (`@X119`, owner 2026-08-14).  ⚠ It does **not** forbid a stat, and `DESIGN.md` § 8's noncombatant player is the part that did not move: the crew get better at WORKING, nobody gets better at FIGHTING |
| Ask HOW a crew member improves, or add a skill to the lattice | [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2a (`@X124`) — ⚠⚠ **it is a LOOP**: practice raises the skill fast and its **two** statistics slowly, and each statistic lifts **four** skills, so practising `repair` quietly improves build, operate, mine, boost, hack and scrounge.  ⚠ Measured off `archive/gameplay.data`: 12 skills, 6 statistics, 24 edges, **diameter 2** — no crew member ever dead-ends.  ⚠⚠ A new skill must take one of the **four free statistic pairs**, never duplicate an occupied one: `combat` and `scout` are already twins (both observe+stamina) and that is why they permeate to 5 where everything else reaches 6 (`@X128`) |
| Ask what a player LEAVES BEHIND on a planet, or design persistent world content | ⚠⚠ **The planet accumulates other people's play** (`@X174`, [`docs/DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) § 20) — an abandoned base, a **cache** (`@X173`) and a stranded helper's **homestead** (`@X171`) are ONE shape: things a player leaves that persist, another player finds, and nobody authored.  ⚠⚠ **It is the cheapest content in the design and the only kind that TEACHES** — a homestead where you thought nothing could live shows what is possible, which is the only lesson `@X137`'s no-tutorial ruling permits.  ⚠ A cache is **hard to find, hard to crack, and cracking should be LOUD**; ownership stays legible because it feeds reputation.  ⚠ All three want per-planet persistence and none wants netcode beyond a shared store |
| Ask what happens to a helper LEFT BEHIND, or whether crew move between players | [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2e (`@X168`-`@X170`) — ⚠⚠ **another player may pick them up, and if the relation is frankly bad they stay with their rescuer.**  ⚠⚠ **Crew move between players but NEVER as goods**: not a transaction but a choice, through abandonment and rescue rather than the market — *you cannot buy somebody's crew, you can only be the one who came for them*.  ⚠ **No new mechanic**: `plans/14`'s retrieval path is shipped.  ⚠⚠ **The default must be RETURN** or multiplayer reads as being robbed — which makes returning somebody a favour with no reward, so it buys **reputation** instead.  ⚠ Degrades to single player with no second design: the rescuer is a competitor (`@X160`) |
| Add a RELATIONSHIP, loyalty or affinity mechanic | ⚠⚠ **Not as a bar** — [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2e (`@X131`).  A relationship here is **a LEDGER of what happened**, and it is the same mechanism as the practice loop: the crew get better at what they DID and closer through what they WENT THROUGH.  ⚠ The player never raises it — there is nothing to farm and no bar to draw; their only influence is **who they send together and who they come back for**.  ⚠ The history already exists (`@X132`): who retrieved whom, who was left behind at force-launch, who boarded.  ⚠ A mechanical effect must be COVERAGE (a faster handoff), never a threshold, an unlock or a gate (`@X133`).  ⚠ Counts + landmarks, never a log (`@X134`) |
| Add a SETTING, an option, a config or a mode | ⚠⚠ **THE HOUSE RULE: a fine default, and options that surface when reached for** (`@X261`, [`docs/DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) § 11).  ***Every system works with NO input, and its controls appear only when the player reaches for them*** — in the world, through channels that already exist, never a popup or a settings screen.  ⚠⚠ **The test is `@X262`**: does it work with no input, and does its depth surface when reached for?  If it must be configured before it works, it needs a tutorial — and there is none.  ⚠ Three failures: **a bad default** (a strawman punishing the player for not reading), **surfaced too eagerly** (a tutorial in a different hat — the trigger is the player REACHING, never a timer), and **invisible when wanted** (the reach must have something to meet) |
| Ask about a TUTORIAL, onboarding, tooltips or a hint system | ⚠⚠ **There is no tutorial** (`@X137`, [`docs/DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) § There is NO TUTORIAL) — no tutorial mission, no first-run overlay, no tooltip layer.  ⚠ It is affordable because **most of the game has no key to learn**: § Position triggers means driving over loot picks it up and standing at a tower repairs it, so play mode is five keys plus the mouse.  ⚠⚠ Three of a tutorial's four jobs were already answered, and the **recon window is the sandbox** (`@X138`, `@X022`) — nobody built it for that.  ⚠⚠ The fourth job, *what to do first*, belongs to the crew's remarks, which makes them **the onboarding** rather than a convenience |
| Add a KEY BINDING, or a mechanic that needs explaining | ⚠⚠ **The key table is a design BUDGET** (`@X139`) — every key is a thing the player must discover unaided, so a new binding needs an argument, not a row; `tests/09_i1_bindings.loft` pins the row count and goes red by design.  ⚠⚠ **And the test for any new mechanic is: can a player find this by playing around?**  One that needs explaining needs redesigning, because there is nowhere left to explain it — design it in spatial terms first (§ Position triggers).  ⚠ Two hazards are already named (`@X140`): **Q** (wall-paint) is the sharpest, since a player who never presses it never builds, and **Shift** (boost) is the only way out of a sealed base |
| Ask whether a crew member may be WRONG | ⚠⚠ **No — nothing they say is false** (`@X147`, `@X148`).  They are **unreliable narrators**, and the skew is entirely **selection, emphasis and omission**: which true things they mention, how much weight each gets, and what goes unsaid.  ⚠ *"Nobody came for that tower"* is true; the skew is offering it as THE reason when the wall was also mis-sited where they never went.  ⚠⚠ **So the player can trust every sentence and still not have the picture** — the worst case is incomplete, never misleading, and the build is a **filter over a true event list** with no fiction generator.  ⚠ Bias misleads in degree never direction, and is consistent per character, which makes calibrating your own crew a progression that accumulates on the dominant axis.  ⚠⚠ And they **report, never PRESCRIBE** (`@X149`): what to do differently next run is the player's to derive |
| Add anything to the POST-MISSION screen, or ask what a debrief may say | [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2f (`@X144`-`@X146`) — the player may **optionally** ask the crew what they thought, which is PULL where remarks are PUSH.  ⚠⚠ **The fence RELAXES here**: the base is finished, so there is no live question to short-circuit and the crew may CONCLUDE freely — and what the player carries away is **rules-knowledge**, the axis that is supposed to accumulate.  ⚠⚠ **But never a SCORING SCREEN** — `DESIGN.md` § 14 has no fail screen and says a bad run is *felt across the sequence, not announced*.  Opinions, not a grade; and no dialogue tree, skippable in one action.  ⚠ Note the **empty chair**: a helper left behind is not there to be debriefed |
| Ask how a player DISCOVERS an advanced option | ⚠⚠ **Through the debrief, as a person being wasted** (`@X255`, [`docs/PROGRESSION.md`](PROGRESSION.md) § P2f) — a helper says they spent the base doing work beneath what they are good at.  ***The player is never told the system exists; they are told a PERSON WAS WASTED.***  ⚠ It obeys every debrief rule: points without concluding, says nothing false, is **stumble-able**, and one speaks not six.  ⚠⚠ **It must read as a person underused, never as a SCORE** (`@X256`) — the difference is feeling *told off* versus feeling *you let somebody down*, and only the second produces the curiosity that finds the feature.  ⚠⚠ And it is **a tutorial the first time and a defection warning the tenth** (`@X257`) |
| Ask who does a job, or whether helpers pick their own work | ⚠⚠ **SEMI-AUTOMATIC BY DEFAULT, SPECIALISED BY ATTENTION** (`@X252`, [`docs/DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) § 9) — helpers find work themselves, and the player may **go to one** and give them specific tasks, after which **that one behaves less generally**.  ⚠⚠ **Auto is a DEFAULT, not a ceiling**, which is why it does not violate the assignment pillar.  ⚠⚠ **The pillar survives because DIRECTING COSTS A TRIP**: your attention is the scarce resource, so *which one you specialise IS the decision*.  ⚠ It is a **ratchet**, so a crew accumulates shape — and it reinforces the practice loop for free, since a helper you keep directing to repair becomes a repairer.  ⚠⚠ **Over-specialising tells you**: narrowed helpers go idle more, so they speak up |
| Judge a task-assignment, priority-list or automation idea | ⚠⚠ **ASSIGNMENT IS A PILLAR** (`@X197`, [`docs/DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) § 9) — *never totally clear what they should do, and always more tasks than helpers*.  ⚠ It is § What kind of game this is at the **management layer**: the scarce thing is a person, and the cost is everything they are not doing.  ⚠⚠ **The scarcity is structural** — growth creates its own demand, so the 6-helper cap is the **mechanism** and not a limit to overcome.  ⚠⚠ **The test is `@X198`: does this make ONE of the four axes dominate?** (value-kind, information, growth, position).  ⚠⚠ A **priority-ranked task list** and **auto-assignment** both delete the pillar — the second is the quality-of-life feature most likely to be requested |
| Ask WHO speaks, or when a crew remark fires | ⚠⚠ **The helper with NO TASK assigned** (`@X142`) — the player gives the orders, so an idle worker reporting in is what a foreman hears.  ⚠ It needs no stall detector: it reads a task list that already exists and **cannot misfire on a parked player who is repairing a tower**.  ⚠⚠ **It self-calibrates** — assign everyone and there is silence; on landing nobody has orders, so the onboarding remark falls out of the rule.  ⚠ **Idleness picks WHO and WHEN, skills pick WHAT**: the ex-miner says *"there's ore on that ridge"*, never *"I am idle"*.  ⚠ **One speaks, never six** (`@X143`), and assigning work is the in-fiction off switch |
| Add a DIALOGUE UI, a topic menu or a conversation system | ⚠⚠ **There is no question list anywhere in this design** (`@X156`).  Presence IS the interaction: the player drives over, stays a moment, and hears what that person has to say — which is `DESIGN.md` § 11 § Position triggers applied to conversation, *activated through motion, not by typing*.  ⚠⚠ **The player chooses WHO and WHEN; the crew choose WHAT** — so push and pull are one mechanism with two doors (the game picks the speaker when a helper is idle; the player picks by driving over).  ⚠ The line appears **near them in the world**, never in a panel, and it is the same verb as repairing a tower.  ⚠ Applies at the station too |
| Deliver LORE, or ask how the player ever learns the setting | ⚠⚠ **The crew are the only channel** (`@X152`) — `SETTING.md` is ~2 300 lines and nothing else can carry it: `@X137` refuses a tutorial, § HUD refuses an overlay, and `@X102`'s knowledge tree only covers what is found ON the planet.  ⚠⚠ **The split is Dryopea vs everywhere else**: novices about this planet (`@X150`), natives of the setting — *they can explain where you all came from and not where you are*.  ⚠ Lore arrives as **overheard chatter, never delivered** — two crew disagree about the cordon and the player picks it up in pieces.  ⚠ The crew carry the cordon's public story and military reality; **the hidden truth stays the planet's** (`@X155`) |
| Write a crew member's LINE, or give a class a voice | ⚠⚠ **Class decides what they notice; VOICE decides how they say it** (`@X157`) — a miner talks in effort, a scout in terse position reports, a hacker obliquely about how a thing is built, a researcher hedged and unwilling to commit past the evidence.  ⚠⚠ **Personality reaches wording and emphasis, NEVER the facts** — a blunt person and a hedging one report the same thing, so the player gets the same information from either, just faster from one.  ⚠⚠ **Build it as a FILTER, not a matrix** (`@X158`): domain picks a true thing to say, voice colours the phrasing — additive authoring, never (class × personality × situation).  ⚠ A personality may change how pleasant a source is, never whether it is a source; clashing temperaments go in **chatter**, never into work efficiency |
| Ask what a crew member is allowed to KNOW | ⚠⚠ **No more than the player — they are on this planet for the first time too** (`@X150`).  Already the fiction: Centaur Mining does **automated asteroid mining** and took a contract it has no experience of.  ⚠⚠ **The distinction is NOTICING vs KNOWING** — a past job gives trained ATTENTION (a miner's eye goes to the ground), never world knowledge (what Dryopea's ground does).  *"That rock is fractured, on an asteroid I'd not cut near it"* passes; *"this is bad rock, don't build here"* does not.  ⚠⚠ **So the no-concluding fence needs no discipline: they cannot conclude because they do not know either.**  ⚠ They still onboard, by drawing attention rather than explaining.  ⚠ And they **learn alongside the player** (`@X151`), so their knowledge stays a SUBSET of what the player had access to |
